The House

Three generations at the same loom, in the same room off Sharia el-Muizz.

Farris began as a single bolt of linen and a stubborn belief: that a garment should outlive the season that made it. Sixty years on, nothing about that has softened.

Every piece leaves Cairo with a hand-inked lot number, the weaver's initials, and a repair promise that has no expiry date. We make fewer things. We make them once.

The Khamseen Collection

Named for the wind that carries the desert into the city for fifty days. Six pieces, each cut to move the way that wind does.

Where the colour comes from

We do not invent our palette. We collect it.

Carnelian from the Eastern Desert. Faience blue from the kilns at Qena. The exact ochre of the river at the twentieth minute after sunset.

The Making

Forty-one pairs of hands, in order.

01

The Bolt

Giza 96 cotton is bought on the field, before the gin. We take the second picking only — longer staple, fewer breaks, a hand that gets better with washing.

02

The Dye Pit

Six sunken vats of mineral and madder in the courtyard. No two lots match, and we have stopped apologising for it — that variance is the signature.

03

The Loom

Four wooden looms, all older than the house. Nine metres a day, on a good day, from a weaver who will not be hurried by anyone, including us.

04

The Needle

Gold-wrapped thread, couched by hand along the placket and hem. Between eleven and forty hours per piece, depending on how much of the lotus you asked for.

05

The Mark

Lot number inked, weaver's initials stitched inside the left seam, and the piece is yours — with a repair promise that does not expire.
