What this looks like

A trade turns against you in the first minute and the thought isn't “I might be wrong.” It's “the market hasn't caught up yet.” You add to it. You're not managing a position — you're refusing to be corrected by a candle.

Your journal has gaps exactly where the lessons live. The trade that would teach you the most is the one you never wrote down, because writing it down would mean admitting it happened the way it happened.

When someone who knows more than you points at the mistake, you explain why the trade made sense instead of asking what it cost you. A single bad trade rarely ends an account. Refusing to admit it was bad, and doubling the size to prove it wasn't — that's what does.

A better system still needs someone willing to be shown it was wrong in the moment. You've already decided that person isn't you.

Proof it isn't just theory

I'm Abrihym Aobad. For years I blew account after account and never made a dollar — not from a lack of strategy, but because I hadn't governed the self doing the trading. I stepped away, came back to my deen, and started applying what Islam had already been teaching: patience, restraint, sincerity, gratitude. Same charts, same markets — a different self placing the trades. Then the payouts started. I don't share these to boast. I share them because for years this was the proof I never had — that the shift was real in the account, not just in my head.
