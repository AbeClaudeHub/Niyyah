The story

From a street in Unayzah to Morris Park Avenue

Kyan was never built on one flagship. It was built on repetition — the same cup, the same welcome, the same hour of the evening, four hundred times over. Morris Park is where that habit landed in New York, on a stretch of the Bronx the neighbourhood calls Little Yemen, where Arab coffee culture was already at home long before we arrived.

2009 · Unayzah

A single small shop opens in Al-Qassim, in the Saudi interior, serving coffee and tea to a neighbourhood that keeps coming back.

The Kingdom

The shop becomes a chain. Kyan spreads across Saudi Arabia and grows into the largest privately held coffee and tea retailer in the country.

The Gulf & beyond

Franchising carries the brand into Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, Jordan, the UAE and Iraq — sixth largest branded coffee chain in the Middle East.

2024 · The Bronx

Kyan opens at 689 Morris Park Avenue: the first Saudi Arabian coffee chain to open a store in the United States, and our first outside the Middle East.

Blue on white, after the ceramic tradition that runs from Qassim to Iznik — the colour scheme of the room, and of this page.

The one they come back for

The Spanish Latte

Espresso poured over sweetened condensed milk, then cold milk and ice — served long, in a glass, so the layers hold for the first three seconds and then don't. It is the drink the reviews are actually about, the reason the line runs down Morris Park Avenue after 2pm on a Saturday.

The room

Built for the second hour

Most coffee shops are designed for the first fifteen minutes. This one is designed for what happens after — the table you don't get asked to give back.

Open until midnight

Seven days a week, from seven in the morning. Late is not an exception here — it's the point. Expect a wait on weekend afternoons after two.

Halal, all of it

Every item on the board, front to back, with no asterisks and nothing you need to ask twice about. It is why Morris Park adopted the place so quickly.

Little Yemen

Morris Park Avenue already had the grocers, the bakeries and the late tables. Kyan didn't introduce Arab coffee culture to this street — it joined it.
