The story behind the book

It started with the worst forty minutes of my trading life.

ن

I had a rule, in my own handwriting, taped to the bottom of my monitor: two losses and I'm done. One Tuesday I took the second loss — and then I watched myself read that rule, look straight at my own ink, and break it anyway.

I moved my stop. Then I moved it again. By the time it was over I'd lost more than in the previous three weeks combined. The thought that arrived wasn't what a bad trade. It was: who was that?

That was the real horror — not the money, but discovering there was someone living inside me I didn't know. Who didn't respect my rules. Who showed up only under heat, and who, in the moment that mattered most, was stronger than me.

I could have done what most traders do: decided the problem was technical, bought another course, blamed my edge. But I hadn't had a strategy problem. I'd overridden a strategy that was fine. The failure happened in the six inches between my brain and my hand — a place no charting software has ever reached.

When I went looking for who had mapped that gap most precisely, I didn't find it in modern psychology. I found it in my own tradition — the 1,400-year-old Islamic science of the nafs, the self. This book is what happened when I stopped trying to become a better trader and started trying to master the one person who kept losing.

— Abrihym Aobad, founder of Niyyah
