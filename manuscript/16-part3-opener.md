# PART III

# THE FIVE DISEASES

---

The scholars of the heart didn't speak in vague terms about "negativity" or "bad habits." They were physicians of the soul, and like all good physicians they named diseases precisely — because a disease correctly named can be treated, and a disease left vague festers under a hundred comfortable euphemisms.

They spoke of *amrāḍ al-qulūb,* the diseases of the hearts. Al-Ghazālī gave a full quarter of his *Iḥyāʾ* to them — the destructive traits of the soul, each anatomized and each prescribed its cure — and Ibn al-Qayyim charted the same terrain in his *Madārij al-Sālikīn,* tracing how a disease of the heart enters, takes hold, and is treated. They catalogued these illnesses, traced their symptoms, mapped their progression, and prescribed their cures with a specificity that puts most modern self-help to shame. They understood that the heart, like the body, can sicken, and that a sick heart produces sick behavior as reliably as a sick body produces fever.

The trader's heart is susceptible to five of these above all others. They aren't random. They're precisely the diseases a screen full of moving money is built to inflame: **impatience, greed, fear, pride, and despair.** Every catastrophic trade you've ever made traces back to one or more of them. They are the five faces of the lower self in the chair.

In the chapters ahead we put each on the examination table — how it shows itself in your trading, the machinery that drives it, what the Qur'an and the Prophets and the early Muslims revealed about it, and the specific, practical training that treats it.

This is the diagnostic heart of the book. Read it the way a patient reads an honest doctor's notes — not to admire the writing, but to find his own name on the page.

You will. Probably more than once.
